Region: Central India
Language: English
Theme: friendship

## Folk Tale 004

In the verdant heart of Central India, where ancient Sal trees pierced the sky and the Narmada river flowed with the wisdom of ages, lived an unlikely pair of friends: Hiranand, a graceful deer with eyes like melted chocolate, and Chiranjeev, a wise old crow whose plumage shimmered like polished obsidian. Their bond was as deep as the roots of a Banyan tree and as enduring as the hills themselves. Hiranand, with his swift legs, would often graze in the sun-dappled clearings, while Chiranjeev, with his keen sight, kept watch from the highest branches, warning his friend of any approaching danger. They shared stories of the forest, secrets of the sweetest berries, and the tranquil joy of simply being together.

One fateful morning, as the first rays of dawn painted the forest in hues of gold and rose, Hiranand was enjoying a lush patch of grass near the forest's edge. Chiranjeev was soaring high above, completing his morning reconnaissance. Suddenly, a sharp pain shot through Hiranand's leg – he had stepped into a hunter's snare, cleverly hidden beneath fallen leaves. Panic seized him; he struggled desperately, but the rope tightened, digging cruelly into his flesh. His heart pounded like a drum, and despair clouded his gentle eyes. From his lofty perch, Chiranjeev's sharp eyes caught the commotion below. With a cry of alarm, he swooped down, his concern a palpable presence.

"Oh, my dear Chiranjeev!" whimpered Hiranand, tears welling. "I am caught! This is the end for me!" Chiranjeev landed on a nearby branch, his intelligent eyes surveying the trap and the surrounding area. He saw the fresh tracks of the hunter, named Vyadha, who would surely return soon. "Do not despair, my friend," croaked Chiranjeev, his voice calm despite the urgency. "Listen carefully. When Vyadha arrives, you must pretend to be dead. Lie absolutely still, hold your breath, stiffen your body, and keep your eyes closed. Do not move, no matter what. I will fly above him, cawing loudly, to distract him just as he reaches you."

Hiranand, though trembling with fear, trusted his wise friend implicitly. He took a deep, shaky breath and then lay motionless, his body stiff, his eyes shut, feigning death with all his might. Soon, the heavy footsteps of Vyadha could be heard approaching. His eyes gleamed with greed when he saw the "dead" deer in his snare. "Ah, what a magnificent catch!" he muttered, rubbing his hands together in satisfaction. He approached the deer, his mind already conjuring images of a grand feast.

As Vyadha bent down to examine his prize and untie the snare, Chiranjeev burst into a flurry of loud, agitated caws, circling just above the hunter's head. Annoyed, Vyadha instinctively looked up, trying to shoo away the noisy bird. In that split second of distraction, Chiranjeev gave a quick, almost imperceptible signal to Hiranand. The deer, who had been holding his breath and every muscle taut, exploded into action. With a powerful lurch, he ripped free from the loosely held snare and bounded away with the speed of the wind, disappearing into the dense foliage before Vyadha could even react.

The hunter stood frozen, his mouth agape, staring at the empty snare and the rapidly vanishing tail of the deer. His catch had vanished right before his eyes! He let out a roar of frustration that echoed through the forest, but it was too late. Hiranand, panting but free, ran until he reached a safe, familiar grove deep within the woods. Moments later, Chiranjeev landed beside him, his wise eyes alight with relief and triumph. They stood together, the deer and the crow, their hearts filled with gratitude for a friendship that had proven stronger than any snare, and cleverer than any hunter.

**Moral of the story:** True friendship, combined with quick wit and mutual trust, can overcome the greatest of dangers.
