## Folk Tale 014

**Region:** Assam
**Theme:** Bravery

In the verdant embrace of Assam, where the mighty Brahmaputra river snaked through emerald valleys and the foothills of the Himalayas stood sentinel, lay the tranquil village of Rongpur. Life here was a gentle rhythm, dictated by the seasons and the bounty of paddy fields. Among its people lived a young man named Bikram, known not for his physical prowess, but for his quiet demeanor and an uncanny knack for observing the natural world. While others toiled in the fields or gathered by the communal fire, Bikram often wandered to the fringes of the dense jungle or sat by the riverbank, his eyes keenly studying the intricate dance of life around him. Some villagers, mistaking his contemplation for idleness, often called him a dreamer.

The peaceful existence of Rongpur was shattered by the arrival of Lai Aathi, a colossal rogue elephant, notorious for his tuskless might and destructive rampages. Lai Aathi was a legend of terror across several villages; his sudden appearances meant flattened huts, destroyed crops, and sheer panic. Hunters from nearby areas had tried to capture or deter him, but the cunning beast seemed impervious to their tricks, always vanishing into the impenetrable jungle as swiftly as he appeared. Fear gripped Rongpur, and every rustle in the bamboo groves sent shivers down the villagers' spines, paralyzing them with helplessness.

While the elders debated erecting stronger barricades and the young men spoke of desperate hunts, Bikram remained silent. Instead, he spent his days observing from a distance, hidden amongst the foliage, watching Lai Aathi’s patterns. He noticed the elephant had a peculiar habit: it always seemed to approach the village through a narrow, winding ravine that led from the deeper forest, especially after the searing afternoon heat. He also observed its immense attraction to the sweet, fermented jaggery water used in many homes during festivals – a scent that seemed to override its usual caution. Most importantly, he noted a slight limp in its left foreleg, a subtle weakness that might make it less agile in tight turns.

With these observations, Bikram formulated a plan, daring and unconventional. He proposed to the villagers that he would not fight Lai Aathi with spears or traps, but with understanding and a trail of sweet temptation. He would lure the elephant through the very ravine it favored, using large pots of jaggery water, and guide it far into the uninhabited depths of the forest, away from their homes forever. The villagers scoffed. "You, a mere boy, against the legendary Lai Aathi?" an elder, Borun Da, exclaimed, urging him to abandon such a suicidal folly. But Bikram, with a quiet resolve in his eyes, insisted, "Strength alone cannot defeat cunning, nor can fear overcome fear. We need intelligence and courage."

That evening, as the sun dipped below the horizon, painting the sky in fiery hues, Lai Aathi's trumpeting reverberated through the air, signaling his approach to Rongpur once more. Panic erupted. But before anyone could react, Bikram, armed only with a small oil lamp and a long bamboo stick (not for striking, but for gentle prodding), stepped forward. With a deep breath, he walked towards the thundering presence, calling out to the colossal beast, not aggressively, but with a strangely calm, almost gentle tone. He directed the elephant's attention to the first pot of jaggery water he had carefully placed at the ravine's entrance. The sweet, intoxicating scent drew Lai Aathi in.

Slowly, cautiously, Bikram began to lead the mighty creature. He moved ahead, placing pot after pot, his lamp casting dancing shadows, his heart thumping like a drum. The elephant, drawn by the irresistible aroma and perhaps sensing no threat from the lone, calm figure, followed the winding path deeper into the ravine, further and further away from Rongpur. The villagers watched, breathless, from their hiding places, their fear slowly transforming into awe as they witnessed the incredible spectacle of a young man, through sheer bravery and wit, guiding the legendary menace into the enveloping darkness of the jungle. By dawn, an eerie silence had settled. Lai Aathi was gone.

Relief washed over Rongpur like a refreshing monsoon rain. The next morning, the villagers emerged, their cheers echoing through the valley. They surrounded Bikram, not just celebrating his extraordinary bravery, but marveling at his wisdom and compassion. Borun Da embraced him warmly, tears in his eyes. "We sought to fight strength with strength," he said, "but you, Bikram, fought fear with courage and insight. You have taught us that true bravery is not the absence of fear, nor the display of brute force. It is the courage to face a challenge with intelligence, compassion, and a clear mind, even when all others doubt you."
