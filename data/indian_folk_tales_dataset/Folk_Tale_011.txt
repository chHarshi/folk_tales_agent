**Title: Folk Tale 011**
**Region: Telangana**
**Theme: Selflessness**

In the heart of Telangana, nestled amidst vast fields of cotton and paddy, lay the village of Palampet. Its people were known for their resilience and their devotion to the ancient Ramappa Temple nearby. But one scorching summer, their resilience was tested to its limits. The monsoon failed entirely. The mighty Godavari river, usually a lifeblood, dwindled to a sluggish stream far away. Village wells cracked under the sun's relentless gaze, and the once-green fields turned to parched, dusty canvases. Despair, like a heavy monsoon cloud, hung over Palampet.

Among the suffering villagers lived Lakshmi Amma, a woman whose face was etched with the wisdom of eighty monsoons. She was revered for her gentle spirit and her unwavering kindness. Unlike others, Lakshmi Amma’s small compound, tucked away behind a grove of tamarind trees, still held a hint of green. For generations, a tiny, almost secret spring had trickled beneath her kitchen floor, providing just enough water to keep her small vegetable patch alive and fill her clay pots daily. It was her family's precious secret, a fragile lifeline in the unforgiving land.

As the drought worsened, Lakshmi Amma watched her neighbours struggle. Children cried for water, mothers walked miles only to return with empty vessels, and the cattle moaned in thirst. Her heart ached. Could she sit idly by, knowing she had a hidden source, however small? The thought pricked her conscience sharper than any thorn. One evening, after much prayer and contemplation, Lakshmi Amma made a decision that surprised even herself. She would open her cherished spring to the entire village.

The next morning, Lakshmi Amma stood before the village council, her voice frail but clear. "My children," she announced, "I have a small spring. It is barely enough for my own needs, but it is there. I offer it to you all. If we share it wisely, with care and without waste, perhaps it can sustain us until the rains bless us again." A wave of murmurs rippled through the crowd. Some were skeptical; how could one tiny spring help hundreds? Others were overwhelmed by her selflessness. But seeing the genuine warmth in her eyes, the villagers understood her sacrifice. They organized immediately: the strongest men dug a shallow channel from her home to a communal trough, the women took turns managing the distribution, ensuring every household received a fair share, and the elders watched over the spring day and night to prevent any spillage.

Miraculously, the tiny spring, though not overflowing, seemed to flow with renewed vigour under the collective care. The water, rationed with such profound respect and shared with such mutual understanding, stretched further than anyone could have imagined. Lakshmi Amma, who had offered her last resource, found her own pots filled by grateful neighbours. The act of sharing created a bond stronger than the drought itself. And as if acknowledging their newfound unity and selflessness, a week later, dark clouds gathered on the horizon. The heavens opened, and the life-giving rain poured down, drenching the parched earth and filling the wells once more.

The drought eventually broke, and Palampet bloomed anew. But the memory of Lakshmi Amma's selfless act, and the way the entire village came together, remained etched in their hearts. The communal trough, fed by a now robust spring, became a symbol of their unity. From that day forward, the people of Palampet understood that true prosperity lay not just in abundant water or fertile land, but in the boundless wellspring of compassion and the strength found in selfless sharing.

**Moral:** A single act of selflessness, when met with collective responsibility and care, can inspire a community and bring forth untold blessings.
