**Folk Tale 018**
**Region: East India**
**Theme: Honesty**

In the heart of a lush, green land, where the great river Ganga’s tributaries snaked through fertile plains, lay the village of Kamalpur. Life here was simple, dictated by the rhythm of the seasons and the toil of the earth. Among its humble inhabitants was Ratan, a young woodcutter with a spirit as pure as the morning dew. Though poverty was a constant companion in his small, thatched hut, Ratan's hands were always busy, and his heart was always kind. He was known not for his wealth, but for his unwavering integrity, a quality as rare and precious as a gem.

One sweltering afternoon, as Ratan ventured deep into the Sal forest bordering Kamalpur, seeking sturdy branches for firewood, his gaze fell upon an unusual sight beneath the gnarled roots of an ancient banyan tree. It was a small, intricately embroidered velvet pouch, partially buried in the damp earth. His heart gave a lurch as he picked it up; the weight was unmistakable. Peeking inside, he gasped – it was overflowing with shimmering gold coins, catching the dappled sunlight and glinting like a king’s treasure. For a moment, the vastness of his own want, the dreams of a better life for his ailing mother, flashed before his eyes. This fortune could change everything.

But Ratan’s honest nature quickly prevailed over the siren call of wealth. "This is not mine," he murmured, his brow furrowed with resolve. "It belongs to someone who has lost it, and I must return it." He tied the precious pouch securely to his waist and, abandoning his woodcutting for the day, set off towards the bustling market town of Gauripur. He asked every merchant, every vendor, every passerby, describing the pouch, hoping to find a clue. Many laughed, some advised him to keep it, saying "Fortune favours the brave, Ratan!" But Ratan remained undeterred. Finally, an old spice merchant remembered overhearing Sethji Basanta, the wealthiest merchant in Gauripur, lamenting the loss of a distinctive velvet pouch of gold coins just yesterday.

With newfound hope, Ratan made his way to Sethji Basanta’s grand mansion. The guards, seeing his simple attire, initially dismissed him, but Ratan’s earnestness eventually persuaded them. Sethji Basanta was a shrewd man, known for his sharp mind as much as his riches. He listened to Ratan’s story with a wary eye. "A pouch of gold coins, you say?" Sethji questioned, leaning forward. "How many coins were there, young man? And what did the pouch look like?" He watched Ratan carefully, his gaze probing, for he had been tricked by opportunists before. He even subtly suggested that his pouch had contained *more* coins than Ratan might have found, a common trick to deter dishonest finders.

Ratan, however, remained calm and truthful. He accurately described the intricate embroidery on the velvet, the size, and the approximate number of gold coins he had seen, not a coin more, not a coin less. He did not claim more, nor did he ask for any reward, simply stating, "Sir, I found what was lost, and it is my duty to return it." Sethji Basanta’s stern expression softened. He saw the genuine honesty shining in Ratan’s eyes, untouched by greed. This young man had walked away from a fortune simply because it wasn’t his. Deeply moved, Sethji retrieved his accounting ledger, verifying Ratan's description. It was indeed his lost treasure.

Overwhelmed with gratitude and admiration, Sethji Basanta not only took back his pouch but also insisted that Ratan accept a generous reward – a small bag of gold coins, a sum far greater than Ratan could earn in a year. "Your honesty, young man, is worth more than all the gold in my vaults," Sethji declared. Ratan humbly accepted, using the coins to provide comfort for his mother and to start a small, thriving business selling high-quality firewood. The story of Ratan's integrity spread far and wide, echoing through the plains of East India, a testament that honesty, like a strong root, anchors a person in dignity and ultimately brings forth the sweetest fruits.
