**Folk Tale 010**
**Region: Tamil Nadu**
**Theme: Honesty**

In the sun-drenched village of Thanjavur Palayam, nestled amidst emerald paddy fields watered by the generous Kaveri, lived a young man named Kannan. Though his small hut, thatched with palm leaves, offered little comfort, Kannan’s heart was as rich as the fertile earth around him. He toiled from dawn till dusk, assisting farmers in their fields, his strong arms and gentle spirit known to all. He owned little but cherished his reputation for unwavering honesty more than any earthly treasure.

One sweltering afternoon, as Kannan walked the dusty path winding through the shimmering fields, heading home after a long day’s work, his eyes caught a glint near a cluster of ancient banyan roots. Bending down, he found a small, exquisitely embroidered silk purse. Its unexpected weight told him it was not empty. His fingers trembled as he opened it, revealing a cascade of glimmering gold coins – eighty, he counted carefully. His first thought was not of personal gain, but of the distress the owner must be feeling at such a significant loss.

Kannan spent the rest of the day and evening inquiring amongst the villagers and market vendors, describing the purse and its contents. He even stood by the spot he found it, hoping someone might return to search. News of a lost silk purse quickly reached the ears of Chettiar Durairaj, a wealthy and influential merchant known for his vast stores of silks and spices, and occasionally, for his shrewdness in dealings. Chettiar Durairaj promptly announced to the village council, the *Panchayat*, that the lost purse was indeed his, claiming it contained a hundred gold coins.

Upon hearing this, Kannan, clutching the precious purse, respectfully approached Chettiar Durairaj. "Ayya," he began, "I found a silk purse on the path to the market. I believe it might be yours." Chettiar Durairaj, a cunning smirk playing on his lips, extended his hand. "Indeed, it is mine! I have been most anxious. Give it here, boy." Kannan, however, hesitated. "But Ayya, the purse I found contains eighty gold coins." Chettiar Durairaj feigned outrage. "Eighty, you say? My purse contained a hundred gold coins! Are you implying I am mistaken, or worse, that you have pilfered twenty of my precious coins, you scoundrel?" Kannan's heart sank at the unjust accusation, but he stood his ground, his gaze unwavering.

The dispute was brought before the village Panchayat, presided over by the venerable Thalaivar, an elder renowned for his sagacity. Kannan calmly recounted his find, stating the purse held exactly eighty gold coins. Chettiar Durairaj, with a show of indignation, reiterated his claim that his lost purse contained a hundred coins. The Thalaivar listened patiently, his eyes keenly observing both men. Then, with a thoughtful nod, he declared, "Chettiar Durairaj, since your lost purse contained a hundred gold coins, and the purse Kannan has found contains only eighty, then clearly, this cannot be your purse. Your true purse, with its hundred coins, must still be lost. Kannan, you are an honest young man. Keep this purse of eighty coins safe, and continue your search for its rightful owner, for it is certainly not Chettiar Durairaj’s."

A stunned silence fell upon the gathering. Chettiar Durairaj’s face turned crimson with shame. His deceit was exposed for all to see. With his head bowed, he confessed his lie, admitting he had hoped to profit from Kannan's honesty. The Thalaivar, sternly but kindly, chastised the merchant. To Kannan, he turned with a warm smile. "Kannan, your honesty shines brighter than any gold. Since no other claimant for a purse of eighty coins has come forward, and Chettiar Durairaj, in his greed, has disowned it, I declare these eighty gold coins rightfully yours, as a reward for your impeccable character. May your integrity inspire us all." The villagers applauded, celebrating Kannan's virtue.

And so, Kannan, through his unwavering honesty, not only gained a fortune but also the profound respect and admiration of his entire village. His story echoed through Thanjavur Palayam for generations, a timeless reminder that while falsehoods may offer fleeting gains, it is honesty alone that brings true and lasting rewards.
