Title: Folk Tale 020
Region: Central India
Language: English
Theme: friendship

## Folk Tale 020

In the heart of Central India, where ancient sal trees kissed the sky and a shimmering pond mirrored the endless blue, lived an unlikely pair of friends: Chanchal, the swift and graceful deer, and Dheeman, the slow but wise tortoise. Chanchal, with his agile legs and keen senses, roamed the vast forest, while Dheeman preferred the quiet depths of the pond. Despite their stark differences in pace and habitat, their bond was profound. They would often share stories and observations at the pond's edge, Chanchal recounting the day's adventures in the woods, and Dheeman offering calm, thoughtful insights. Their friendship was a testament that true connection transcends all superficial distinctions.

One sunny afternoon, as Chanchal grazed peacefully in a sun-dappled clearing, his joy was abruptly shattered. Unbeknownst to him, a cunning hunter named Vyadha had laid a strong net trap hidden beneath dry leaves. Chanchal stepped right into it, and in an instant, his legs were ensnared in the thick ropes. Panic seized him, and he struggled desperately, but the more he thrashed, the tighter the net gripped him. His heart pounded with fear, and he let out a piteous cry for help, his voice echoing through the silent forest, hoping that some kind soul might hear his plight.

Miles away, Dheeman was basking by the pond when Chanchal's distressed call reached his ears. Without a moment's hesitation, he began his long, arduous journey towards his friend. Chanchal, meanwhile, continued to struggle, growing weaker and more despondent with each passing moment. When he finally spotted Dheeman slowly approaching, he felt a flicker of hope, but it quickly dimmed. "Oh, my dear Dheeman!" he lamented, "You are so slow! By the time you reach me, the hunter will surely have arrived, and my fate will be sealed!" Dheeman, however, maintained his steady pace, his wise eyes reassuring Chanchal, "Patience, my friend. Haste often leads to mistakes. Let wisdom guide our actions."

Reaching the trapped deer, Dheeman quickly assessed the situation. He instructed Chanchal, "My friend, you must play dead. Hold your breath, close your eyes, and appear utterly lifeless. Trust my plan." With these words, Dheeman began to gnaw patiently at the thick ropes of the snare. His teeth, though not designed for such work, were steady and persistent. Slowly, painstakingly, he began to weaken the strong knots, one fibre at a time, while Chanchal, following instructions, lay motionless, his heart pounding but his body still.

Soon, the hunter Vyadha, guided by the sounds of Chanchal's earlier struggles, arrived at the clearing. His eyes gleamed with satisfaction upon seeing the apparently "dead" deer in his trap. But his surprise turned to bewilderment when he noticed Dheeman, the tortoise, diligently gnawing at the ropes. "What is this?" he muttered, astonished by the unusual sight. As Vyadha momentarily diverted his attention, surprised by the tortoise, Dheeman swiftly chewed through the last few strands. Sensing his moment, Chanchal, with a powerful surge of adrenaline, broke free from the now weakened net and bolted away into the depths of the forest, disappearing in a flash.

Furious that his prize had escaped, Vyadha cursed loudly. He turned his attention to Dheeman, intending to catch him instead. "You little nuisance!" he growled, reaching out to grab the tortoise. But Dheeman, though slow on land, was perfectly adapted to his watery home. With surprising speed, he scuttled towards the nearby pond and plunged into its depths, vanishing beneath the lily pads and murky surface before the hunter could lay a hand on him. Vyadha was left standing empty-handed and frustrated, his trap useless and his hunt fruitless.

Later that evening, Chanchal and Dheeman reunited at their usual spot by the pond, safe and sound. Chanchal profusely thanked Dheeman for his quick thinking and unwavering loyalty. Dheeman, ever humble, simply smiled, glad to see his friend alive. Their ordeal had only strengthened their bond, proving that true friendship isn't about being alike, but about supporting each other through thick and thin, combining different strengths to overcome adversity.

**Moral:** True friendship is a treasure where diverse strengths unite, and cleverness can often triumph over brute force.
