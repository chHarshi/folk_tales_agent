## Folk Tale 002

**Region:** East India
**Theme:** Honesty

In a small, verdant village nestled along the banks of the mighty Padma River, where the air hummed with the songs of fishermen and the rustle of paddy fields, lived a humble man named Gobindo. Day in and day out, Gobindo cast his nets, his lean frame a familiar silhouette against the rising sun. He was a simple man, known for his quiet nature, his diligent work, and a profound honesty that was as clear as the river water itself. Though poverty was a constant companion, Gobindo never grumbled, finding contentment in the rhythm of his life and the integrity of his heart.

One particularly sweltering afternoon, as Gobindo pulled in his net after a long morning, he felt an unusual resistance. It was far heavier than any catch of fish. With considerable effort, he dragged the net to the muddy bank, his eyes widening in astonishment at what lay entangled within the meshes. It was not a fish, but a beautifully crafted wooden box, adorned with intricate carvings of dancing peacocks and blooming lotuses. His heart pounded with curiosity as he unlatched it. Inside, nestled on a bed of velvet, lay a pouch overflowing with glistening gold coins and a necklace that shimmered with emeralds, catching the sunlight like drops of dew.

A gasp escaped Gobindo’s lips. This was wealth beyond his wildest dreams, enough to transform his meagre existence, to repair his dilapidated hut, to provide for his family for generations. For a fleeting moment, temptation flickered in his mind, painting vivid pictures of a life free from toil. He imagined the comfort, the respect, the end of all his worries. But just as quickly, the image of his mother’s kind face, and her often-repeated wisdom – "What is not rightfully yours, however tempting, will bring only sorrow" – flooded his thoughts. His conscience, clear and unwavering, settled the matter: this treasure belonged to another.

Determined to find the true owner, Gobindo did not hide his discovery. Instead, he carefully re-secured the box and, with the heavy weight of the treasure and his resolve, walked straight to the village headman, Pradhanji. Gobindo recounted his tale honestly, describing the box and its contents in detail. Pradhanji, a wise and respected elder, listened intently, his eyes reflecting admiration for Gobindo’s forthrightness. He immediately dispatched messengers to neighbouring villages and put out word far and wide that a valuable lost treasure had been found, hoping to reach the rightful owner.

Days turned into a week, and then two, but no one came forward to claim the treasure. Just as despair began to creep into Gobindo's heart, a wealthy merchant from a distant town, Sethji Ramnath, arrived in the village. He had heard the news through his trade network. With an air of urgency, he approached Pradhanji, describing a box exactly matching Gobindo’s find, mentioning the peacocks and lotuses, the gold coins, and the unique emerald necklace. Pradhanji then summoned Gobindo, who, without a moment's hesitation, produced the box. Sethji’s face lit up with relief and joy as he confirmed every detail.

As Sethji Ramnath reached out to take his cherished possessions, his eyes fell upon Gobindo, who simply smiled, his act of honesty complete. Overwhelmed by such integrity, Sethji refused to let Gobindo’s virtue go unrewarded. "My honest friend," he declared, "this treasure is but a fraction of what you deserve. Your honesty has saved me from great despair, for this box contained not just wealth, but heirlooms of immense sentimental value." Despite Gobindo's humble protests, Sethji insisted he accept a generous reward – a sum far greater than Gobindo had ever seen, enough to build a comfortable life. Furthermore, deeply impressed, Sethji offered Gobindo a position of trust in his thriving trading business, recognising that such honesty was a far greater asset than any gold.

And so, Gobindo's life was transformed, not by the allure of ill-gotten gain, but by the quiet power of his unwavering honesty. He became a respected and prosperous man, not just within his village, but throughout the region. His story became a timeless legend whispered by the Padma's banks, reminding all that true wealth lies not in what one possesses, but in the integrity of one’s heart.

**Moral:** Honesty is a virtue that always brings its own rewards, often greater and more lasting than any material gain obtained through deceit.
