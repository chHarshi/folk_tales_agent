**Folk Tale 017**
**Region: South India**
**Theme: Courage**

In the verdant embrace of the Western Ghats, where coconut palms swayed in rhythm with the gentle breeze and the River Kaveri sang its ancient songs, lay the serene village of Poomalai. Here lived a young girl named Lakshmi, known throughout the village for her soft voice and delicate hands, ever busy weaving fragrant jasmine garlands for the temple deity. She was often perceived as timid, her eyes usually downcast, preferring the quiet company of her flowers to the boisterous games of other children. Yet, beneath her gentle exterior lay a heart that few had reason to notice, much less understand.

A shadow, however, soon fell upon the sun-drenched paths of Poomalai. A massive, solitary tusker, a magnificent wild elephant known to the villagers as Kalabhairava, the Black Terror, had strayed from the deeper forests. Kalabhairava was not inherently malicious, but its immense size and insatiable hunger led it to raid the precious plantain and sugarcane fields, trampling crops and instilling a deep fear in the hearts of the villagers. Despite their bravery, the men of Poomalai were hesitant to confront the beast directly, for harming a wild creature was against the sacred traditions they had inherited from their ancestors.

One scorching afternoon, as the village children played innocently near the cool waters of the temple tank, the unthinkable happened. Kalabhairava lumbered into the village itself, its heavy footsteps shaking the very earth. Panic erupted as villagers scattered, screaming in alarm. Amidst the chaos, a small child, Maya, Lakshmi’s younger sister, stood frozen in terror, clutching her worn rag doll, directly in the tusker's path. The giant beast, engrossed in reaching for a low-hanging bunch of ripe jackfruit, had not noticed the tiny figure below its massive head. Every adult was paralyzed, too afraid to move, too afraid to intervene.

From behind the gnarled roots of an ancient banyan tree, Lakshmi watched in horror. Her little sister was in mortal danger. In that terrifying moment, a surge of fierce, protective love, stronger than any fear she had ever known, coursed through her veins. Without a moment's hesitation, she seized a small, ceremonial drum, usually beaten with gentle reverence during temple festivals. With all her strength, she began to pound it, not a rhythm, but a series of loud, jarring thumps, running directly towards the elephant from the side, her small voice rising to an uncharacteristic shout, "Hey! Go away! Shoo, Kalabhairava!"

The sudden, unfamiliar clamor and the sight of the tiny girl rushing towards it bewildered the colossal elephant. It paused, its massive ears flaring out in surprise, and let out a low rumble of confusion. Lakshmi, though her legs trembled beneath her, stood her ground, continuing to beat the drum, her gaze unwavering, fixed on the retreating giant. Kalabhairava, more startled than angered, slowly backed away from the jackfruit tree. With a final, questioning trumpet, it turned its massive form and lumbered away, out of the village and back towards the safety of the deep forest, leaving behind only scattered leaves and a collective gasp of relief. Maya, released from her terror, ran straight into Lakshmi's trembling arms.

The villagers, initially stunned by Lakshmi’s extraordinary act, rushed forward, embracing her with tears in their eyes. The elder Muthuswami, his voice thick with emotion, declared, "Our little Lakshmi, who weaves flowers with such delicate grace, has shown us the heart of a lioness! She saved Maya, and in doing so, taught us that true courage is not measured by size or strength, but by the strength of conviction in one's heart." From that day forward, Lakshmi was no longer merely the quiet girl; she was revered as 'Veera Lakshmi' – the Brave Lakshmi.

**Moral:** True courage is not the absence of fear, but the ability to act despite it, especially when protecting others. It resides in the most unexpected hearts and shines brightest in times of dire need.
