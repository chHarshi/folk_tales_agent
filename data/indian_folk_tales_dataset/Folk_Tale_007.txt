# Folk Tale 007

In a quaint village nestled by the meandering Rupnarayan River in Bengal, lived a widow named Aparna. Her tiny hut, with its mud walls and thatched roof, was modest, but within it resided a spirit as boundless as the verdant paddy fields that stretched to the horizon. Aparna possessed little in the way of worldly goods, yet her heart overflowed with a generosity that knew no bounds. She shared her meagre handful of rice with hungry street dogs and always had a kind word for every weary traveller, her gentle smile a comforting balm in the bustling village life.

One fierce monsoon evening, as the sky wept torrential rains and the wind howled like a hungry wolf, a tiny, drenched bulbul bird was flung from its precarious nest. It landed with a soft thump near Aparna’s doorstep, its delicate wing bent at an unnatural angle, its small body shivering uncontrollably. Aparna, stepping out to secure her leaking roof, spotted the helpless creature. Her heart, ever tender, ached at its plight. Without a moment’s hesitation, she scooped up the bird, bringing it into the dim warmth of her hut.

Aparna carefully tended to the injured bird. She fashioned a soft bed from an old cloth, gently splinted its wing with a thin twig, and offered it tiny grains of rice steeped in water. To keep it warm, she sacrificed a significant portion of her precious lamp oil, letting the flame burn brighter through the long, chilly night. Many villagers would have dismissed a mere bird as trivial, but for Aparna, every life was sacred, every creature deserving of compassion. She whispered comforting words to the bulbul, treating it with the same care she would a lost child.

Days turned into a week, and under Aparna’s unwavering care, the bulbul slowly regained its strength. Its wing healed, and soon it was hopping playfully around her small hut, its cheerful chirps filling the quiet corners that had once echoed with Aparna’s solitude. She shared her simple meals with it, her own portions often smaller to ensure her tiny companion thrived. Her neighbours, though fond of Aparna, sometimes marvelled at her devotion to a creature that could offer no material return.

Finally, the day came when the bulbul was ready to fly. Aparna carried it to her open window, a pang of bittersweet sadness in her chest, yet her heart swelled with joy for its freedom. With a joyful flutter, the bird launched itself into the clear morning sky. But before disappearing into the azure, it circled back once, dipping low to Aparna’s window. From its beak, it dropped a single, iridescent feather – a feather unlike any Aparna had ever seen, shimmering with all the colours of the rainbow. Then, with a final, melodic trill, it vanished from sight.

Aparna, touched by the gesture, tucked the feather into her saree. Later that day, grim news spread through the village: the community well, their lifeblood, was drying up, and a severe drought threatened their crops and livelihoods. Despair settled over the village like a heavy shroud. Aparna, gazing at her own wilting vegetable patch, felt a deep ache in her soul, not for herself, but for the collective suffering. She thought of the parched fields, the thirsty cattle, and the worried faces of her neighbours. As she silently wished for rain, not for her own benefit but for all, her fingers unconsciously brushed the iridescent feather.

Suddenly, the air around her tingled. The sky, which moments before had been a relentless blue, began to darken, not with storm clouds, but with a gentle, soft haze. Then, a cool, fragrant mist began to fall, turning into the most exquisite, life-giving drizzle. It wasn't a violent downpour but a steady, nurturing rain that soaked into the dry earth, bringing immediate relief. The village erupted in joyful shouts, believing it a divine miracle. Aparna, clutching the shimmering feather, knew it was more than just luck. She never spoke of the feather, but her heart was lighter than the newly watered soil. Her small garden flourished, and her spirit remained perpetually bright, a testament to the unseen blessings that grace a generous soul.

**Moral:** Kindness, given freely and without expectation, is a powerful force that often returns to us in the most unexpected and beautiful ways, enriching not only our own lives but the world around us.
