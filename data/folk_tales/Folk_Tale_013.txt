**Folk Tale 013**
**Region:** Maharashtra
**Theme:** Compassion

In the undulating embrace of the Sahyadri foothills, nestled in a small village of Maharashtra, lived a humble farmer named Vithoba. His fields were modest, his dwelling simple, and his pockets often light, yet Vithoba possessed a richness that few could claim: a heart overflowing with kindness. He shared his meager harvest with his neighbors, offered a cool drink to weary travelers, and always spoke with a gentle word, believing that every living being, great or small, deserved respect and care.

One scorching afternoon, as Vithoba toiled under the relentless sun in his sparse field, he stumbled upon a sight that tugged at his tender heart. Lying amidst the dry clumps of earth was a tiny sparrow, its wing bent at an unnatural angle, clearly broken. The little bird chirped faintly, its eyes wide with fear and pain, utterly helpless and exposed to the harsh elements and lurking predators. Most farmers, focused on their demanding livelihoods, might have simply averted their gaze, but Vithoba could not. His compassionate nature would not allow him to leave such a fragile life to its fate.

Gently, with hands calloused from years of farming but remarkably delicate, Vithoba scooped up the injured bird. He carried it carefully back to his humble home, where his equally kind wife, Laxmi, made a soft bed of cotton for the creature in a small basket. Vithoba fashioned a tiny splint from a twig and a strand of thread, carefully binding the sparrow's wing. Day after day, he shared crumbs of his own *bhakri* (a coarse flatbread, staple of Maharashtra) and drops of precious water, tending to the sparrow with unwavering devotion, speaking to it in soft, comforting tones as if to a child.

His neighbor, Ganpat, a man known for his shrewdness and practicality, often observed Vithoba's efforts with a shake of his head. "Vithoba," Ganpat would scoff, "Are you truly so foolish? You, who struggle to feed yourself and Laxmi, waste your precious time and meager food on a useless bird? What good will it ever do you?" Vithoba would merely offer a gentle smile. "Ganpat," he’d reply softly, "Compassion is its own reward. My heart would not allow me to abandon a life in need. To care for another, however small, brings a peace that money cannot buy." Ganpat would simply shrug and walk away, convinced of Vithoba's impracticality.

Weeks passed, and under Vithoba's tender care, the sparrow slowly but surely healed. One crisp morning, with a strong, melodious chirp of profound gratitude, it fluttered its newly mended wings and soared out through the open window, disappearing into the vast, blue sky. Vithoba felt no sorrow at its departure, only a quiet joy for its restored freedom. That year, the monsoon rains were sparse, and despair settled upon the village as fields withered and wells began to dry. Yet, miraculously, Vithoba's small plot, the very ground where he had found the injured sparrow, flourished. His grains grew plump, his vegetables lush, and his well, usually the first to run dry, held water long into the season.

The entire village was astonished by Vithoba's unexpected prosperity amidst the widespread hardship. Ganpat, witnessing Vithoba’s bountiful harvest, could not help but remember the farmer's unwavering compassion for the tiny bird. He wondered if there was an unseen connection, a subtle ripple of karma. Vithoba, ever humble, simply smiled, understanding that true richness lay not in accumulated wealth but in the boundless kindness of one's heart.

The story of Vithoba teaches us that true wealth lies not in what we possess, but in the compassion we show to all beings. For a kind heart, no good deed ever goes unrewarded, even if the reward comes in unexpected ways and at unforeseen times.
