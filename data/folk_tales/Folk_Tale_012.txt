Title: Folk Tale 012
Region: Uttar Pradesh
Language: English
Theme: friendship

Namaste, my dear listeners, gather close and let me weave for you a tale from the ancient land of Uttar Pradesh, where the sacred Ganga flows and the earth whispers stories of old. In the bustling village of Mangalpur, nestled amidst sun-drenched fields and mango groves, lived two inseparable friends: Gopal, a diligent farmer with hands weathered by toil, and Kishan, a master potter whose nimble fingers shaped miracles from humble clay. Their bond was as strong as the roots of the banyan tree and as pure as the river water – they shared laughter and sorrow, dreams and dilemmas, always shoulder to shoulder, a true testament to the spirit of companionship.

One year, however, the skies turned unkind. The monsoon clouds, usually so generous, withheld their bounty. The sun beat down relentlessly, scorching the fields, turning Gopal’s vibrant green crops to brittle brown dust. His well, once gurgling with life, became a dry, cracked pit. Debts piled up like storm clouds, and despair began to settle heavy in Gopal's heart. He spoke of selling his small plot of land and moving to a distant city, his spirit broken, his hope fading like the evening light, unable to face the ruin that stared him in the face.

Kishan watched his friend's plight with a heavy heart. Though his own pottery sales had dwindled, he still had a small store of his finest creations. Among them was 'Shanti', a magnificent indigo-glazed vase, adorned with intricate peacocks – the most exquisite piece he had ever crafted, intended as a legacy for his future children. But seeing Gopal’s misery, Kishan knew what he must do. "Do not despair, my brother," he told Gopal, his voice firm, "for as long as I breathe, your fields shall not lie fallow. I shall take Shanti to the great city of Lucknow, where discerning eyes will recognize its worth, and return with enough to save your land."

The journey to Lucknow was long and perilous. Kishan, carefully cradling Shanti wrapped in soft cloth, walked for days under the scorching sun, his feet growing weary, his throat parched. He faced dusty roads, suspicious travelers, and the gnawing fear that his precious cargo might break. There were moments of doubt, when hunger pangs twisted his stomach and his limbs ached for rest, but the image of Gopal's hopeful face spurred him onward, each step a prayer for his friend's well-being, keeping his resolve unwavering.

Finally, Kishan arrived in the grand city, a kaleidoscope of sights and sounds. He found a renowned merchant who, upon seeing the unparalleled artistry of 'Shanti', immediately recognized its value. The merchant bought the vase for a handsome sum, far exceeding Kishan's humble expectations. With the money carefully secured, Kishan began his return journey, his heart lighter than a monsoon cloud, eager to share the good news with Gopal. He walked with renewed vigour, the weight of the silver in his pouch a small burden compared to the joy in his soul.

When Kishan returned to Mangalpur, a week later, Gopal was overwhelmed with emotion. He embraced his friend, tears streaming down his face as Kishan poured out the silver coins. With that money, Gopal paid his debts, bought new seeds, and when the rains finally arrived, his fields bloomed once more, richer and greener than ever before. Their story became a legend in Mangalpur, a shining example for generations. For, as the wise elders would say, 'Though riches may come and go like the seasons, true friendship is a timeless treasure, a sacred bond that can overcome any hardship, brighter than gold and stronger than stone.'
