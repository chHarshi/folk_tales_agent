**Folk Tale 003**
**Region: West India**
**Theme: Selflessness**

In the sun-baked plains of West India, nestled amidst rocky outcrops and thorny bushes, lay the small village of Roopnagar. Life there was a constant dance with the elements, but none was as feared as the relentless sun that often brought prolonged droughts. While the land around Roopnagar withered, year after year, there lived a humble farmer named Devraj whose small patch of land remained an inexplicable emerald jewel. His bajra crop, though modest, always stood tall and green, sustained by a secret, perennial spring that bubbled quietly beneath his ancestral plot.

The latest drought was the harshest in living memory. The village well, once a lively hub, had dwindled to a muddy puddle. The cattle grew weak, the children’s laughter faded, and the fields lay cracked and barren, mirroring the despair in the villagers’ eyes. Only Devraj’s patch held any semblance of life, a stark and painful contrast. Whispers grew – of Devraj’s good fortune, of his hidden secret, and of the injustice that one man’s plot flourished while an entire village suffered.

One sweltering evening, the village elders, their faces etched with worry, approached Devraj’s modest home. The Sarpanch, a man of great wisdom and a heavy heart, spoke not of accusation, but of desperation. "Devraj," he began, his voice raspy, "your field, it alone thrives. We know not how, but the life in it calls to us. Our children starve, our wells are dry. If there is a way, any way at all, for Roopnagar to drink again, we beg of you, consider it." Devraj listened, his heart heavy, knowing the truth of his hidden spring and the immense sacrifice they were unknowingly asking for.

Devraj spent the night in profound contemplation. To share his spring meant his own patch, his family’s sole sustenance, might wither. It was his inheritance, his security, his only shield against the harsh realities of their land. Yet, the image of thirsty children and despondent faces of his neighbours haunted him more than the thought of his own loss. By dawn, his decision was firm. With a pickaxe and shovel, under the first blush of morning, he began to dig a trench, not around his field, but away from it, towards the communal well of Roopnagar.

Hour after hour, Devraj toiled under the merciless sun, until his hands bled and his muscles screamed. As the sun reached its zenith, a thin stream of clear, cool water began to trickle from his trench into the parched village well. A gasp of wonder rippled through the gathered villagers, followed by a surge of disbelief and then overwhelming gratitude. They watched as Devraj, exhausted but resolute, connected his precious secret spring to the lifeblood of their community. His own field, now deprived of its exclusive bounty, visibly began to droop.

The villagers, witnessing Devraj’s immense selflessness, felt a collective wave of shame for their earlier whispers and then an even greater wave of resolve. Inspired by his sacrifice, they did not just drink from the well; they rallied. They helped Devraj repair his own field, strengthening the channels, ensuring the water flowed fairly to both his land and the common well. They worked together to deepen the communal well, creating small bunds and channels to harvest rainwater for the future. Roopnagar, revitalized by water and united by Devraj’s selfless act, began to flourish again, not just in crops, but in spirit.

From that day on, Roopnagar thrived. The once-secret spring, now shared by all, seemed to yield more abundantly than ever before, as if blessed by the village’s unity. Devraj never regretted his decision; his personal loss was immeasurably outweighed by the collective gain and the enduring respect and love of his people.

The moral of the story is: True prosperity and lasting happiness are not found in what one hoards, but in what one selflessly shares with others.
